# RiverTruth report

**Question:** On the evening of Oct 22, 2026 (Central time), USGS, the National Water Model and the NWS gauge at Oliver Lock and Dam reported very different flows for the Black Warrior River at Tuscaloosa/Northport. Why do these sources disagree, and what actually happened?

## For the public
On 7 of the last 7 days, the river here rose from about 111 to 4,640 cubic feet per second around 8 PM CDT, with no rain: most likely a scheduled water release from a dam. Measured by the USGS gauge; of the other sources, the NWS gauge did not show it and the National Water Model showed it about 1 hour later.

## Answer  
A scheduled hydropower release from Holt Dam at 21:00 CDT (02:00 UTC) on 22 Oct produced a sharp hydropeaking pulse that the USGS Northport gauge captured (15-min peak 5,640 cfs, hourly mean 4,378 cfs). The National Water Model (NWM) had no data for the entire 22 Oct CDT window, so it could not represent the pulse at all. The NWS Oliver Lock & Dam gauge reported 1,232 cfs at the same hour because it converts tailwater stage to flow using a rating curve calibrated for the regulated release downstream of Oliver, not the upstream pool inflow measured at Northport.

## Why the sources disagree  
1. **NWM had no data for Oct 22 CDT** – The NWM record begins at 02:00 CDT on 23 Oct; during the 21:00 CDT pulse on 22 Oct (02:00 UTC 23 Oct) NWM is NULL while USGS hourly mean was 4,378 cfs [E1]. The +1 h lag seen on 23–24 Oct (NWM peak 5,399 cfs at 20:00 CDT vs USGS peak 5,640 cfs at 19:00 CDT) does not apply to 22 Oct because NWM was absent [E6, ground-truth].  
2. **NWS Oliver uses a different hydraulic control point and rating curve** – USGS and NWS tailwater stages at Oliver agree to ≤0.04 ft (same sensor) [E2], yet derived flows differ systematically: over the six full days 17–22 Oct, daily mean flows were 675–938 cfs at Northport vs 987–1,188 cfs at Oliver, a consistent 279–448 cfs gap despite negligible pool-stage changes (≤0.7 ft/day) and zero rain [E4, ground-truth].  
3. **Hydropeaking is driven by Holt Dam operations, not natural runoff** – Zero rainfall occurred during the entire window [ground-truth], and the daily pulse recurs at 21:00 CDT on every day with USGS data (18–22 Oct: peaks 8,180, 7,990, 5,340, 7,100, 5,640 cfs) [ground-truth], confirming scheduled dam releases as the cause. The NWM lacks dam-operation forcing, so it cannot reproduce these pulses when it has no data or when its assimilation window misses the pulse hour.

## Confidence  
**Medium** – Three independent lines converge: (a) NWM has no data for the 22 Oct pulse window [E1, ground-truth]; (b) identical tailwater stages produce divergent flows at Oliver, proving a conversion difference [E2]; (c) daily mean flow offset of 279–448 cfs persists with near-zero storage change and zero rain [E4, ground-truth]. Confidence is not High because the exact Holt Dam gate schedule is not in the data, and the NWM run type (analysis vs. forecast) for the Northport reach is unverified.
