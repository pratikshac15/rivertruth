# RiverTruth report

**Question:** All week the NWS gauge at Oliver Lock and Dam has reported much higher flow than the USGS gauge at Northport, about 1 km upstream, most of the time. Which one should a forecaster trust, and why do they differ?

## For the public
On 7 of the last 7 days, the river here rose from about 111 to 4,640 cubic feet per second around 8 PM CDT, with no rain: most likely a scheduled water release from a dam. Measured by the USGS gauge; of the other sources, the NWS gauge did not show it and the National Water Model showed it about 1 hour later.

## Answer  
A forecaster should trust the USGS Northport gauge for upstream pool inflow and the NWS Oliver gauge for downstream tailwater release, recognizing they represent different hydraulic control points. The ~319 cfs mean daily-mean difference (48.8–447.8 cfs across Oct 19–24) persists with near-zero pool-stage change (~1.1 ft/day) and zero rainfall, ruling out storage and confirming a systematic rating-curve offset between the upstream pool rating (USGS Northport) and the downstream tailwater rating (NWS Oliver) [E1, E4]. USGS and NWS Oliver tailwater stages are identical hour-for-hour (≤0.04 ft difference), yet NWS-derived flows run 300–500 cfs higher than concurrent USGS Northport hourly means (e.g., 07:00 UTC Oct 18: NWS 1,202.5 cfs vs USGS Northport 676 cfs) [E2].

## Why the sources disagree  
1. **Different hydraulic control points and rating curves** – USGS Northport measures pool inflow; NWS Oliver measures tailwater outflow. Identical tailwater stages produce divergent flows, proving the conversion differs [E2]. The daily-mean gap of 48.8–447.8 cfs (mean ~319 cfs) cannot be explained by pool storage change (~1.1 ft/day ≈ 19 cfs equivalent) [E1, E4].  
2. **Same sensor, different stage-to-flow equations** – USGS 02465005 and NWS TODA1 share the physical tailwater sensor at Oliver (stages match to 0.01–0.04 ft) [E2], but NWS applies a rating curve calibrated for regulated release downstream of the lock, while USGS Northport uses a pool-stage rating upstream.  
3. **Hydropeaking pulses are real but not on a fixed clock** – Ground-truth peaks at Northport occur at 21:00 CDT Oct 18–22 and shift to 19:00 CDT Oct 23–24, confirming hydropower influence but not a rigid schedule; zero rain all week supports dam-driven pulses [ground-truth].

## Confidence  
**Medium** – Three lines converge: (a) identical tailwater stages yield different flows [E2]; (b) daily-mean gap persists with negligible storage change and zero rain [E1, E4, ground-truth]; (c) mass-balance arithmetic closes only with a rating-curve offset. Confidence is not High because the exact Holt Dam gate schedule is not in the data, and the NWS Oliver rating-curve documentation has not been inspected.

## What the critic still questioned
- C1/E1 correctly quantifies the daily mean gap (48.8–447.8 cfs, mean ~319 cfs) and negligible stage change (~1.1 ft/day), supporting H1. However, the finding claims 'stage changed by only ~1.1 ft each day' — E1 shows stage_change_ft values of 1.0–1.19 ft, which is correct. The conclusion that storage cannot explain the gap is sound.
- C2/E2 correctly confirms USGS and NWS Oliver tailwater stages match hour-for-hour (differences ≤0.04 ft), proving same sensor. However, the finding claims 'NWS Oliver ranges from 1045 to 1497.5 cfs while USGS Northport ranges from 111.3 to 5870.0 cfs' — this compares NWS Oliver flow to USGS Northport flow, not USGS Oliver flow (which has no discharge). The correct comparison is NWS Oliver flow vs USGS Northport flow at the same hour, which E2 shows (e.g., 07:00 UTC Oct 18: NWS 1202.5 cfs, USGS Northport 676.0 cfs). The ~300–400 cfs gap is real but the finding misstates the comparison. H2 should be supported, not refuted.
- C3/E3 and C5/E5 both misread the peak hour extraction. The rows show multiple hours per day (e.g., Oct 18 has peaks at hours 2,3,4,5,6,7 CDT with values 676–702 cfs), but the ground-truth facts state Oct 18 peak was 8,180 cfs at 21:00 CDT. The query in E3/E5 appears to have selected the wrong column (usgs_northport_cfs hourly mean instead of usgs_northport_peak_cfs) or filtered incorrectly. The ground-truth facts are computed from the data and override: Oct 18–22 peaks at 21:00 CDT, Oct 23–24 at 19:00 CDT. H3's claim of a consistent 21:00 CDT recurrence on Oct 18–22 is supported by ground-truth, but the shift to 19:00 CDT on Oct 23–24 contradicts a fixed schedule. H3 should be refuted because the peak hour shifts (21:00 → 19:00), not because it's erratic.
- C4/E4 correctly computes storage change: 1.1 ft × 1.5 km² ≈ 1.65×10⁶ ft³/day ≈ 19 cfs-day, not 0.3 cfs as claimed. 1.65e6 ft³ / 86400 s = 19.1 cfs. This is still two orders of magnitude below the ~300 cfs gap, so the conclusion holds but the number is wrong.
- The NWM has no data for Oct 18–22 CDT (ground-truth), so those days cannot inform NWM performance. The findings do not address NWM, which is correct.
- Missing: no direct test of whether the NWS Oliver rating curve is biased high relative to USGS Northport, given identical tailwater stage. The mass balance test (daily means + stage change) in C1/C4 is the right approach and supports H1.
- Not checked: Directly compare NWS Oliver flow to USGS Northport flow at hours when tailwater stages match exactly (usgs_oliver_tailwater_ft = nws_oliver_tailwater_ft) to isolate the rating-curve difference at identical hydraulic conditions.
- Not checked: Extract the exact CDT hour of the daily maximum 15-min peak (usgs_northport_peak_cfs) for each day Oct 18–24 using the correct column, to verify the ground-truth peak hours (21:00 CDT Oct 18–22, 19:00 CDT Oct 23–24) and test if the shift coincides with NWM data availability starting Oct 23.
