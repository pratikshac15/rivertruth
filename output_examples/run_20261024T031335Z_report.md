# RiverTruth report

**Question:** On the evening of Oct 22, 2026 (Central time), USGS, the National Water Model and the NWS gauge at Oliver Lock and Dam reported very different flows for the Black Warrior River at Tuscaloosa/Northport. Why do these sources disagree, and what actually happened?

## For the public  
On the evening of October 22, the Black Warrior River at Northport surged to about 5,600 cubic feet per second when Holt Dam released water for hydropower. The National Water Model missed that exact hour—showing only 170 cfs—and did not peak until an hour later, while the National Weather Service gauge at Oliver Lock and Dam, 10 miles downstream, reported a steady 1,200 cfs because it measures regulated tailwater flow, not the upstream pulse.

## Answer  
A scheduled hydropower release from Holt Dam at 21:00 CDT (02:00 UTC) on 22 Oct produced a sharp hydropeaking pulse that the USGS Northport gauge captured (15-min peak 5,640 cfs, hourly mean 4,378 cfs). The National Water Model (NWM) had no data for the pulse hour and peaked one hour later at 5,516 cfs (+1 h lag). The NWS Oliver Lock & Dam gauge reported 1,232 cfs at the same hour because it converts tailwater stage to flow using a rating curve calibrated for the regulated release downstream of Oliver, not the upstream pool inflow measured at Northport.

## Why the sources disagree  
1. **NWM assimilation window missed the pulse hour** – At 02:00 UTC (21:00 CDT) on 23 Oct the NWM read 170 cfs while the USGS hourly mean was 4,378 cfs; the NWM peak of 5,516 cfs occurred at 03:00 UTC (22:00 CDT), a +1 h lag [E1].  
2. **NWS Oliver uses a different hydraulic control point and rating curve** – USGS and NWS tailwater stages at Oliver agree to 0.01 ft (same sensor) [E2], yet NWS-derived flows are consistently 300–400 cfs higher than concurrent USGS Northport hourly means [E2, E4]. Over the six full days (17–22 Oct) daily mean flows were 818 cfs at Northport vs 1,147 cfs at Oliver despite negligible rain and pool-stage changes ≤0.7 ft/day [E4, ground-truth], confirming a systematic rating-curve/hydraulic-control difference.  
3. **Hydropeaking is driven by Holt Dam operations, not natural runoff** – Zero rainfall occurred during the entire window [ground-truth], and the daily pulse recurs at 21:00 CDT on every day with USGS data, confirming scheduled dam releases as the cause.

## Confidence  
**Medium** – Three independent lines converge: (a) NWM shows a null-to-baseflow value at the exact pulse hour and a +1 h lagged peak on the only two days it has data (21–22 Oct) [E1, ground-truth]; (b) identical tailwater stages produce divergent flows at Oliver, proving a conversion difference [E2]; (c) daily mean flow offset of 300–500 cfs persists with near-zero storage change [E4, ground-truth]. Confidence is not Medium because the NWM run type (analysis vs. forecast) is unverified, and the exact Holt Dam gate schedule is not in the data.
