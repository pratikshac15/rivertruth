# RiverTruth report

**Question:** At NWS gauge AVLA1, do the USGS gauge, the National Water Model and the NWS gauge agree about river flow this week? Where they disagree, why, and which should a forecaster trust?

## For the public  
The USGS gauge at Bevill Lock and Dam reported river height but no flow values at all for the past week, while the NWS gauge (AVLA1) showed a steady regulated flow around 2,200–3,300 ft³/s. The National Water Model only ran for the last two days and simulated flows 5–8 times lower than what the NWS gauge measured.

## Answer  
USGS 02444161 provides stage but zero discharge data for the entire 7-day window (0 of 169 hours) [E1]; NWS AVLA1 is the only continuous flow record, averaging ~3,000 ft³/s daily [Ground-truth]. NWM data exist only for the final 48 hours (Oct 21 17:00 – Oct 23 16:00 CDT) and run 5.5–7.7× lower than NWS AVLA1 (median ratio 7.07) [E2], with no diurnal correlation (Pearson r ≈ 0.12) [E5]. Forecasters should use NWS AVLA1 for flow and USGS only for stage.

## Why the sources disagree  
- **USGS vs NWS flow: fundamental absence** — USGS discharge is entirely NULL (0/169 hours) while NWS AVLA1 reports continuous regulated flow [E1].
- **NWM vs NWS flow: large low bias, no dam-operations forcing** — During the 48-hour overlap, NWM ranged 376–502 ft³/s vs NWS 2,180–3,265 ft³/s; mean NWM 438 ft³/s vs mean NWS 3,008 ft³/s (ratio 0.14) [E2, E5]. NWM peaks near 02:00–03:00 CDT while NWS peaks midday (e.g., 3,265 ft³/s at 11:00 CDT Oct 23) [Ground-truth], confirming NWM does not capture the lock-and-dam release pattern.
- **USGS vs NWS stage: same sensor/datum** — Hourly stage values agree within 0.02 ft across Oct 16–18 [E3], refuting any ~13 ft offset in the *observed* stage. Today's stage (109.0 ft) is 13.0 ft below the NWS action stage (122 ft) because the river is below flood level; that is normal and is not evidence of a datum difference.
- **No consistent diurnal peak in NWS flow** — Daily maximum NWS flow occurs at varying CDT hours (21:00, 07:00, 07:00, 14:00, 15:00, 14:00, 17:00, 11:00) with no single recurring hour [Ground-truth], so the regulated pattern is not a simple fixed-schedule hydropeak.

## Confidence  
**High** — Multiple independent lines converge: USGS flow entirely missing [E1]; NWM available only 48 h with consistent ~7× low bias and phase shift [E2, E5]; USGS/NWS stage match to 0.02 ft [E3]; no fixed diurnal peak in NWS flow [Ground-truth].

## What we could not confirm  
- Whether the NWM series in Zetaris is an analysis/assimilation run or a forecast run — this affects interpretation of latency and capture.
- Precise dam gate/lock operation schedule that drives the observed flow variability.

## What to check next
