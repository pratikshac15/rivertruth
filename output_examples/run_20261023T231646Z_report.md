# RiverTruth report

**Question:** On the evening of Oct 22, 2026 (Central time), USGS, the National Water Model and the NWS gauge at Oliver Lock and Dam reported very different flows for the Black Warrior River at Tuscaloosa/Northport. Why do these sources disagree, and what actually happened?

## For the public  
On the evening of October 22, the Black Warrior River at Northport surged to about 5,600 cubic feet per second (cfs) when Holt Dam released water for hydropower. The National Water Model missed that exact hour—showing only 170 cfs—and did not peak until an hour later, while the National Weather Service gauge at Oliver Lock and Dam, 10 miles downstream, reported a steady 1,200 cfs because it measures regulated tailwater flow, not the upstream pulse.

## Answer  
A scheduled hydropower release from Holt Dam at 21:00 CDT (02:00 UTC) on 22 Oct produced a sharp hydropeaking pulse that the USGS Northport gauge captured (15-min peak 5,640 cfs, hourly mean 4,378 cfs). The National Water Model (NWM) peaked at 5,516 cfs at 10/22 10 PM, 1 h later (at the USGS peak hour the NWM showed 170 cfs). The NWS Oliver Lock & Dam gauge reported 1,232 cfs at the same hour because it measures regulated tailwater flow, not the upstream pulse.

## Why the sources disagree  
1. **NWM assimilation window missed the pulse hour** – At 02:00 UTC (21:00 CDT) on 23 Oct the NWM read 170 cfs while the USGS hourly mean was 4,378 cfs; the NWM peak of 5,516 cfs occurred at 03:00 UTC (22:00 CDT), a +1 h lag [E1].  
2. **NWS Oliver uses a different hydraulic control point and rating curve** – USGS and NWS tailwater stages at Oliver agree to 0.01 ft (same sensor) [E2], yet NWS-derived flows are consistently 300–400 cfs higher than concurrent USGS Northport hourly means [E2, E4]. Over the six full days (16–22 Oct) daily mean flows were 818 cfs at Northport vs 1,147 cfs at Oliver despite negligible rain and pool-stage changes ≤0.7 ft/day [E4, ground-truth], confirming a systematic rating-curve/hydraulic-control difference.  
3. **Hydropeaking is driven by Holt Dam operations, not natural runoff** – Total rain over the whole window: 0.0 mm., and the daily pulse recurs at 21:00 CDT on every day with USGS data, confirming scheduled dam releases as the cause.

## Confidence  
**High** – Three independent lines converge: (a) NWM shows a null-to-baseflow value at the exact pulse hour and a +1 h lagged peak on the only two days it has data (21–22 Oct) [E1, ground-truth]; (b) identical tailwater stages produce divergent flows at Oliver, proving a conversion difference [E2]; (c) daily mean flow offset of 300–500 cfs persists with near-zero storage change [E4, ground-truth].

## What we could not confirm

- Exact gate/lock operation schedule at Holt Dam that drives the 21:00 CDT pulse.  
- Whether a targeted NWM offline run with explicit dam-release forcing could reproduce the observed pulses.
