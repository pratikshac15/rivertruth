Black Warrior River at Tuscaloosa and Northport, Alabama

Every river gauge on the map has up to four sources: a USGS gauge measures the flow, the National Water Model simulates it, the National Weather Service gauge reports its own number, and rainfall is tracked separately. They often disagree, sometimes by more than 20 times, and it is hard to know which to trust.

The River check above answers that in seconds from numbers computed in code. For a full explanation, a team of AI agents investigates: it tests each possible cause against the data in Zetaris, challenges its own findings, and gives an answer you can check.

On this river, flow at Northport has jumped from about 150 to over 5,000 cfs within an hour at 9 PM every evening this week, with no rain: water released from a hydropower dam upstream. Anyone on the water below a dam would feel that surge, even when the model still shows a calm river.

Which rivers near here have the most conflicting numbers?

If you look a river up online you may find three different flows for it: the USGS gauge, the National Water Model and the NWS gauge. This compares them hour by hour over the last 48 hours for the 8 gauges closest to the centre of the map, and ranks the rivers where they disagree most. A gap of 7× means the highest reading is 7 times the lowest: before trusting any one number there, run a River check. It also shows researchers where the national model is off right now. The first comparison of a gauge takes a minute while its sources are registered in Zetaris.
